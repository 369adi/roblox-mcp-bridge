# Reference: modeling with parts
Load it for: building with parts: props, buildings, vehicles, characters, maps

Roblox models here are built from parts by script: clean, studded, classic-Roblox style unless the user asks for something else (smooth, realistic, low-poly). Ask about style and rough size if the request does not say and it matters.

## `build_model(name, code)`
Runs your Luau in Edit mode with a `Model` named `name` already created as `model`, the stud material pack loaded, and these helpers defined. Every part you make with them is parented to `model`, anchored and studded. When your code ends the model is put in `workspace` and the tool returns the part count and size. Building again with the same name replaces the previous model of that name.

```
V3(x, y, z)                                              -- Vector3.new
make(kind, name, size, cframe, colour, variant?)         -- kind: "Part" | "Wedge" | "Corner" | "Cylinder" | "Ball"
block(name, w, h, d, x, y, z, colour, variant?)          -- box standing on (x, y, z): y is its BASE
column(name, diameter, height, x, y, z, colour, variant?)-- upright cylinder standing on (x, y, z)
wheel(name, diameter, width, x, y, z, tyreColour, hubColour) -- axle runs left-right (X); no rotation needed
wall(name, cf, length, height, thickness, openings, colour, variant?)
      -- cf = bottom-centre of the wall, its local X runs along it
      -- openings = { { x =, y =, w =, h = }, ... } measured from the wall's bottom-left; they become real holes
gable(name, cx, baseY, cz, span, length, rise, alongX, colour, variant?)  -- two wedges meeting at a ridge
bone(name, pointA, pointB, w, h, colour, variant?)       -- a limb stretched between two Vector3 points
lamp(name, size, cframe, colour)                         -- thin glowing lens (the only use of Neon)
paint(part, colour, variant?)                            -- recolour a part
```
`colour` is a classic BrickColor name. Use only these: `White, Institutional white, Light stone grey, Medium stone grey, Dark stone grey, Black, Really black, Bright red, Dark red, Bright orange, Neon orange, Bright yellow, Cool yellow, Bright green, Dark green, Earth green, Lime green, Bright blue, Dark blue, Medium blue, Pastel Blue, Bright violet, Royal purple, Hot pink, Pink, Brown, Reddish brown, Dark orange, Nougat, Light orange, Brick yellow`.
`variant` is the stud pattern: `"2022 Universal"` (default), `"2022 Small Stud"`, `"2022 Big Stud"`, `"2022 Weld"`, `"2022 Glue"`.

## Geometry rules that people get wrong
- 1 stud is about 0.28 m. A character is 5 studs tall, 2 wide. A door is 4 wide by 7 tall. A storey is 12 to 14 studs. A car is about 8 wide, 5 tall, 16 long. Size everything against the character.
- Y is up. Build on Y = 0 unless told where to put it. Keep models near the origin or where the user asked; do not build on top of their existing work (check with `search_game_tree` first).
- A `Part` is positioned by its **centre**. Use `block`/`column` (positioned by their base) so stacking is just adding heights.
- A Roblox **cylinder lies along its local X axis**. Wheels with a left-right axle need no rotation. Anything upright (pillar, tree trunk, can) needs `CFrame.Angles(0, 0, math.rad(90))`, which `column` does for you.
- A `WedgePart` slopes up toward its local +Z; its high edge is at +Z. Turn it with `CFrame.Angles(0, yaw, 0)`.
- Rotate with `CFrame.new(position) * CFrame.Angles(rx, ry, rz)` in radians. Order matters: position first.
- Parts must touch or overlap slightly (0.05) where they join. No floating pieces, no gaps you can see through, no two faces in exactly the same plane (it flickers): offset one by 0.05.
- Buildings are **hollow**: floor, four `wall`s with door and window openings, ceiling/roof. A solid block is not a building.

## What makes a model look professional
- **Silhouette first.** Get the big shapes and proportions right before any detail. If the outline does not read as the object, details will not save it.
- **Three levels of detail**: main masses, secondary forms (roof overhang, bumpers, window frames, trims), small accents (handles, lights, bolts). Stop there.
- **Limited palette**: 3 or 4 colours. One dominant, one secondary, one accent, plus a dark for depth. Neighbouring parts differ in colour or in stud variant so edges read.
- **Depth**: inset windows and doors, proud frames and trims (0.2 to 0.4 studs), overhangs on roofs. Flat boxes with painted-on detail look cheap.
- **Symmetry where the real object has it.** Build one side, mirror the X.
- Name every part for what it is (`RoofLeft`, `DoorFrame`), group repeated pieces with loops.
- Part budget: props 10 to 40, vehicles and characters 40 to 100, buildings 80 to 200. Fewer, well-placed parts beat many noisy ones.

## Making it usable in a game
- Everything is anchored by `build_model`. If it must move (vehicle, door, tool, NPC): pick a root part, unanchor the rest and join them with `WeldConstraint`s, set `model.PrimaryPart`. Do this with `execute_luau` after the build, and only if the user wants it to move.
- Doors, buttons and pickups need scripts: load the `scripting` reference.
- Tools need a part named `Handle`.
- Characters meant to animate need a Humanoid rig with `Motor6D` joints: load the `animation` reference.

## Check your build
After `build_model`, call `screen_capture` and look: proportions, floating parts, wrong rotations (wheels lying flat, walls sideways), holes. Fix and rebuild. Two or three rounds are normal.

## Not parts
For organic or high-detail shapes that parts cannot do, tell the user the options: `generate_mesh` (Roblox's generator, if they want that) or a MeshPart they import. Do not fake a detailed mesh with hundreds of tiny parts.
